\begin{abstract}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation (BTFR) represents one of the tightest empirical correlations in extragalactic astronomy. We report a systematic investigation of environmental dependence in BTFR residuals that began with the \textit{falsification} of a single-field hypothesis and led to the discovery of a robust U-shaped pattern. Using the SPARC database, we find that galaxies in both low-density (voids) and high-density (clusters) environments show elevated BTFR residuals compared to intermediate-density (field) environments. For the 175 galaxies carrying a documented environmental classification the quadratic coefficient is $a = 1.33 \pm 0.25$ ($5.26\sigma$, $p < 10^{-6}$); fitted to the full quality-cut sample of 181 galaxies the coefficient is $a = 1.36$. This pattern is \textbf{independently replicated} on 21,834 galaxies from the ALFALFA survey ($a = 0.07$, $p = 0.0065$). We propose a two-field framework (\QOR{}) where antagonistic scalar fields $Q$ (gas-coupled) and $R$ (star-coupled) generate the observed pattern. Analysis of 623,609 IllustrisTNG galaxies confirms a \textbf{discriminating prediction}: gas-poor, high-mass systems show an \textit{inverted} U-shape ($a < 0$), a signature difficult to explain with standard astrophysics alone. We demonstrate that \QOR{} satisfies solar system constraints through environmental screening. This work records the empirical investigation and the reasoning that led to a two-field phenomenological description. It is a consistency test, not a derivation: no forward model links the parameters of the framework to the fitted observables, and no coupling constant is measured here. The peer-reviewed results are published in Slama (2026), Scientific Reports.

\vspace{0.3cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} galaxies: kinematics and dynamics -- dark matter -- modified gravity -- cosmology: theory
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{The Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation}

The Tully-Fisher relation \citep{Tully1977} stands as one of the most robust empirical correlations in extragalactic astronomy. In its baryonic form (BTFR), it states that the asymptotic rotational velocity of a disk galaxy, $\Vflat$, scales with its total baryonic mass, $\Mbar$, according to:
\begin{equation}
\Mbar = A \cdot \Vflat^{\,\alpha}
\label{eq:btfr}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \approx 4$ and $A$ is a normalization constant \citep{McGaugh2000, McGaugh2012, Lelli2016}.

The tightness of this relation is remarkable: despite the diversity of galaxy morphologies, star formation histories, and environments, galaxies follow this scaling with scatter of only $\sim 0.1$ dex. This regularity has been interpreted as a consequence of dark matter halo properties, as a prediction of Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) \citep{Milgrom1983}, or as evidence for more exotic gravitational physics.

\subsection{Environmental Effects in Galaxy Dynamics}

Galaxies inhabit a cosmic web of filaments, walls, voids, and clusters, with local densities spanning several orders of magnitude. Environmental effects are well documented: galaxy morphology correlates with density \citep{Dressler1980}, star formation is quenched in clusters \citep{Gunn1972}, and gas content varies systematically across environments \citep{Haynes1984}.

However, the question of whether the BTFR itself shows environmental dependence has received surprisingly little attention. Several studies have examined BTFR scatter without finding strong environmental signals \citep{Ponomareva2018, Lelli2019}, but these analyses typically searched for \textit{monotonic} trends. The possibility of a \textit{non-monotonic} dependence-where both density extremes behave differently from intermediate densities-has not been systematically explored.

\subsection{Motivation and Approach}

This study originated from a specific theoretical prediction: that a scalar field coupled to matter density would produce monotonic environmental dependence in galaxy dynamics. As we will demonstrate, this prediction \textit{failed}-but the failure itself revealed an unexpected pattern that ultimately led to a richer theoretical framework.

We adopt a hypothesis-driven approach:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Test} a specific prediction (single-field model)
    \item \textbf{Falsify} if data contradicts prediction
    \item \textbf{Investigate} the nature of the discrepancy
    \item \textbf{Reformulate} with a more complete model
    \item \textbf{Validate} through replication and new predictions
\end{enumerate}

This paper documents this conceptual motivation, from initial failure to the discovery of a robust empirical pattern and its theoretical interpretation.

\section{Corrections following peer review}
\label{sec:corrections}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

This document has a history, and it seems more useful to state it than to quietly present a tidied version. The first version was written in December 2025, in the weeks following the discovery of the U-shaped pattern. It was accompanied by three companion documents that went considerably further: one derived the framework from string theory, one proposed a conservation law, one extended the residual method to clinical data. Between February and July 2026, partly through my own verification work and partly through peer review at \textit{Scientific Reports}, most of that theoretical superstructure turned out not to hold.

What follows is a record of what was claimed, what checking it revealed, and what replaced it. I am including it because a reader who finds the December documents, which remain publicly archived, deserves to know which parts survived and which did not, and because the sequence itself is part of the result. The empirical observation that started all of this is unaffected by any of it.

\subsection{The derivation from string theory}

The December version claimed that the cross-coupling $\lambda_{QR}$ between the two fields could be derived from a Type IIB compactification with KKLT moduli stabilisation, and that the derivation gave a value of order unity, matching what the galaxy data seemed to indicate. That match was presented as the first quantitative bridge between string compactification geometry and galactic observations.

It was not a derivation. When the cross-coupling is actually computed from the moduli stabilisation potential rather than estimated dimensionally, the result falls between $10^{-11}$ and $10^{-3}$ depending on the scenario, which is eight to fifteen orders of magnitude below the value that was supposed to have been derived. The order-unity result had been obtained by assuming the masses and the vacuum energy took values that would produce it. The reasoning was circular, and the circularity was not visible to me at the time because I was looking for the agreement rather than testing for it.

The claim is withdrawn. Section~\ref{sec:theory} now states this outcome rather than stopping at the resemblance.

\subsection{The universal coupling constant}

A companion document reported $\lambda_{QR} = 1.23 \pm 0.35$, measured across 1.2 million objects and holding over fourteen orders of magnitude in spatial scale. A single constant governing physics from globular clusters to cosmological structure would have been a remarkable thing.

Fitting the scales jointly rather than separately rejects that hypothesis: $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} = 4.5$, $p = 0.001$. The scales do not share a common value. There was also a plainer problem, which I should have caught before anything was deposited: two of my own documents reported this same quantity as $1.23$ and as $31.4$, a factor of twenty-five apart, and neither referred to the other.

No coupling constant is measured anywhere in this work. The values $C_Q$ and $C_R$ appearing in Section~\ref{sec:discovery} are calibration outputs specific to one sample, not physical constants.

\subsection{The conservation law}

A companion document proposed that the product of matter density and effective gravitational coupling is conserved, $\rho \cdot G_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathrm{const}$, and derived it via Noether's theorem from a rotational symmetry between the two fields.

The Lagrangian does not have that symmetry. An $SO(2)$ rotation between $Q$ and $R$ is broken twice over: by the unequal field masses, and by the quartic cross-coupling term that is the entire mechanism of the model. A Noether current requires the symmetry to hold, and it does not. The relation may still be an interesting empirical regularity, but it is not a conservation law and it was not derived.

The claim is withdrawn, and I would ask that the name attached to it not be used.

\subsection{The significance of the sign inversion}

The December version reported the sign inversion between gas-rich and gas-poor populations at $26\sigma$. That number came from comparing populations selected by their internal properties without controlling for the environments those populations occupy. Gas-poor massive galaxies live preferentially in clusters, so a comparison of that kind measures the difference between two populations and their environments at once, and attributes all of it to the effect being tested.

Controlling for isolation gives $5.3\sigma$ for the full R-dominated sample and $5.2\sigma$ in the densest quartile, with the effect vanishing in the most isolated quartile. Those are the values reported in the peer-reviewed article, and the vanishing in isolated systems is more informative than the significance, since it is what an environmental effect should do and what a population artefact should not.

\subsection{What controlling for internal properties revealed}

This subsection records something that was not wrong before, but that only became visible when the second referee insisted on a stronger test, and it is the most substantive thing peer review contributed to this work.

The referee asked for the analysis to be redone with environment as the primary variable and gas fraction and stellar mass demoted to statistical controls, rather than selecting populations by internal properties first. Doing so on the 53{,}363 galaxies of TNG100-1 confirms the curvature: it stays positive and significant through every model in the hierarchy. But it also shows that the amplitude falls from $+0.079$ with environment alone, to $+0.029$ with controls, to $+0.0095$ once interaction terms are added. The coefficient in the fully controlled model is roughly eight times smaller than in the raw fit.

A substantial part of what looked like an environmental signal is carried by the covariance between environment and the internal properties of galaxies. What survives is real and robustly non-zero, and it should be read as a lower bound on any genuinely environmental effect rather than as a measurement of one. I state this plainly because the raw number is the more impressive one, and it is the smaller number that is honest.

\subsection{The Theory of Everything designation}

One of the December documents described the framework as a candidate Theory of Everything, on the grounds that it contained General Relativity as a limiting case.

It does not. The reduction presented recovers Newtonian gravity with a rescaled coupling constant. There is no light deflection, no perihelion precession, no tensor structure, none of the content that makes General Relativity a theory of gravity rather than a force law. The framework is a phenomenological description of one pattern in one relation. Calling it anything more was a category error, and an obvious one in retrospect.

\subsection{The extension to clinical data}

A companion document applied the residual-diagnostic method to health datasets, reporting signatures in several conditions. That work was exploratory, was never validated against clinical baselines, and was never reviewed by anyone with the relevant expertise. It is not part of this research programme and should not be cited. It remains archived under \texttt{legacy/} for completeness only.

\subsection{What this leaves}

Stripping all of that away leaves less than I had in December, and what remains is what the evidence supports: BTFR residuals depend on environment in a way that is not monotonic; the pattern recurs, qualitatively, across three independent datasets that measure environment differently; the environmental response inverts sign with gas content, and it survives multivariate control; and a two-field description with antagonistic couplings accommodates this where a single-field model with a monotonic coupling does not.

That is a consistency test and not a proof of anything. No forward model connects the parameters of the framework to the numbers we fit, which means the framework is not yet quantitatively falsifiable, and building that model is the next real piece of work rather than a detail to be filled in later.

I want to be clear about how these errors happened, because the mechanism matters more than the list. None of them came from bad data or from a mistake in the empirical analysis. They came from building a theoretical structure on top of a real signal faster than the structure could be checked, and from checking it afterwards in the way one checks something one hopes is true. The verification that caught them was not different in kind from the work that produced them. It was the same work done in the opposite spirit.

The corrections in this section were identified by my own re-derivation and by the referees, before publication and without external challenge. That is the system working as intended, and it cost nothing except a theory I was attached to.

It is worth naming what that theory was, since everything here grew from it. The starting idea was that dividing one entity by another is revealing in itself: that a ratio yields not only a comparison between two quantities, but also a remainder, and that the remainder behaves less like leftover noise than like an identity. What a system expresses, divided by what it retains, leaves something over, and that something might be the part worth reading. The whole of this work began in the thought that learning to read such remainders could tell us something about how the universe is put together, and the residuals of the Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation were simply the first place I looked.

I still find that idea worth pursuing, and saying so is not a reservation about anything above. It is the reason this section exists. An intuition strong enough to organise years of work is precisely the one that needs checking hardest, because its appeal is what makes its consequences feel true before they have been tested. The checks recorded here are what separate the part of it that survived, a measurable pattern in the residuals of one relation, from the parts that did not.

\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:data}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Primary Dataset: SPARC}

Our primary dataset is the Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves (SPARC) database \citep{Lelli2016}, providing high-quality rotation curves and baryonic mass estimates for 175 nearby disk galaxies. For each galaxy, we compute the BTFR residual:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{BTFR}} = \log_{10}(\Vflat) - \left[ a + b \cdot \log_{10}(\Mbar) \right]
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
where $a = -1.058$ and $b = 0.344$ are determined from a global fit to the sample.

Environmental classifications were constructed using known group memberships and morphological proxies following the morphology-density relation \citep{Dressler1980}, yielding four categories: void (27 galaxies), field (107), group (11), and cluster (36).

\subsection{Replication Datasets}

\textbf{ALFALFA:} The Arecibo Legacy Fast ALFA $\alpha$.100 catalog \citep{Haynes2018} provides an HI-selected sample of 21,834 galaxies with W50 line widths and environmental classifications derived from local galaxy density.

\textbf{Little THINGS:} Local Irregulars That Trace Luminosity Extremes, The HI Nearby Galaxy Survey \citep{Hunter2012}, comprising 40 dwarf irregular galaxies with resolved rotation curves.

\subsection{Simulation Data: IllustrisTNG}

We analyze galaxies from the IllustrisTNG cosmological magnetohydrodynamic simulations \citep{Pillepich2018}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TNG100-1:} 53,363 galaxies with $M_* > 10^9 M_\odot$
    \item \textbf{TNG300-1:} 623,609 galaxies, stratified by gas fraction and stellar mass
\end{itemize}

These simulations provide a controlled environment to test whether standard $\Lambda$CDM physics produces the observed patterns.

\subsection{Statistical Methods}

We employ polynomial regression to characterize residual dependence:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{BTFR}} = a \cdot \rho^2 + b \cdot \rho + c
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the environmental density proxy (0 = void, 1 = cluster). A positive quadratic coefficient $a > 0$ indicates a U-shape; $a < 0$ indicates an inverted U-shape.

Robustness is assessed via:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Monte Carlo perturbation (1000 iterations)
    \item Bootstrap resampling (1000 samples)
    \item Jackknife leave-one-environment-out
    \item K-fold cross-validation
    \item Permutation testing
\end{itemize}

\section{Results I: The Failed Prediction}
\label{sec:failure}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{The Single-Field Hypothesis}

We began with a simple theoretical expectation: if a scalar field $Q$ couples to matter density with strength $C_Q$, it should produce a monotonic environmental dependence in BTFR residuals. Specifically, we predicted:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{BTFR}}^{\text{predicted}} \propto C_Q \cdot \rho_{\text{env}}
\end{equation}

For $C_Q > 0$, this predicts higher residuals in denser environments; for $C_Q < 0$, the opposite.

\subsection{Confrontation with Data}

Figure~\ref{fig:qo_failure} shows the dramatic failure of this prediction.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig01_qo_only_failure.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Failure of the single-field (QO) model.} The QO model predicts a monotonic relationship between BTFR residuals and environmental density. Data from SPARC shows the opposite: residuals are elevated at \textit{both} density extremes, contradicting the single-field prediction regardless of the sign of the coupling constant.}
    \label{fig:qo_failure}
\end{figure}

The single-field model fails qualitatively: no choice of $C_Q$ can produce elevated residuals at both low and high density simultaneously. This is not a matter of parameter tuning-the functional form itself is wrong.

\subsection{Forensic Analysis: What Went Wrong?}

Rather than abandoning the project, we conducted a forensic analysis to understand the nature of the failure. Figure~\ref{fig:forensic} reveals the answer.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig02_forensic_analysis.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Forensic analysis of the QO failure.} Detailed examination reveals that BTFR residuals follow a U-shaped pattern: both void and cluster galaxies show positive residuals relative to field galaxies. This non-monotonic behavior cannot be captured by any single-field model with linear environmental coupling.}
    \label{fig:forensic}
\end{figure}

The data reveals a clear U-shaped pattern: void galaxies show elevated residuals, field galaxies show minimal residuals, and cluster galaxies again show elevated residuals. This pattern requires a more sophisticated theoretical framework.

\label{sec:discovery}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Quantifying the Pattern}

Figure~\ref{fig:ushape} presents the formal detection of the U-shaped pattern.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig03_ushape_discovery.png}

Colors indicate environment: void (green), field (blue), group (orange), cluster (red). The quadratic fit (black curve) captures the U-shape with high significance ($a = 1.33 \pm 0.25$, $5.26\sigma$, $p < 10^{-6}$).}
    \label{fig:ushape}
\end{figure}

The quadratic regression yields:
\begin{equation}
a = 1.33 \pm 0.25, \quad \sigma = 5.26, \quad p < 10^{-6}
\end{equation}

Model comparison using AIC/BIC strongly prefers the quadratic model over linear ($\Delta$AIC = 28.8).

\subsection{The Two-Field Solution}

The U-shape suggests that \textit{two} processes with opposite environmental preferences are at work. We propose:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Field $Q$: Dominates in low-density environments (voids)
    \item Field $R$: Dominates in high-density environments (clusters)
\end{itemize}

Both fields contribute to BTFR residuals, but with opposite environmental gradients, producing the observed U-shape.

\subsection{Parameter Calibration}

Figure~\ref{fig:calibration} shows the calibration of the two-field model parameters.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig04_calibration.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Calibration of the \QOR{} model parameters.} Grid search over coupling constants $C_Q$ and $C_R$ to minimize residuals. The optimal values are $C_Q = +2.82$ (positive, gas-coupled) and $C_R = -0.72$ (negative, star-coupled). The sign difference is essential for producing the U-shape.}
    \label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}

The calibrated parameters are:
\begin{align}
C_Q &= +2.82 \pm 0.15 \quad \text{(gas coupling)} \\
C_R &= -0.72 \pm 0.08 \quad \text{(stellar coupling)}
\end{align}

The opposite signs are physically meaningful: $Q$ enhances gravitational effects (positive residuals) while $R$ suppresses them (negative residuals), but they dominate in opposite environments.

These two coupling values are specific to this document. They are the output of a grid search calibrated on the SPARC sample, they are not reported in the peer-reviewed article, and they should not be read as measurements of physical constants: the framework contains no forward model that would give them a scale-independent meaning, and a calibration performed on a different sample returns different values. They are retained here because they document how the two-field description was tuned to the data, which is what this narrative records.

The U-shape is not an artifact of outliers, specific galaxies, or statistical noise.

\subsubsection*{Methodological note on fit procedures}

We have verified that all four combinations of binning schemes (15 or 12 bins, with or without $\sigma$-weighting) preserve the same qualitative behavior: $\Lambda$CDM alone produces a positive coefficient slightly larger than SPARC, and the \QOR{} correction reduces it toward the SPARC value. The specific numerical values $a = 0.045$ and $a = 0.039$ correspond to the methods used in the original pipelines and are retained for traceability with the public code repository.

\section{Theoretical Framework}
\label{sec:theory}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{The \QOR{} Lagrangian}

We propose a two-field effective Lagrangian:
\begin{equation}
\Lqor = -\frac{1}{2}(\partial Q)^2 - \frac{1}{2}(\partial R)^2 - V(Q,R) - C_Q Q \rho_{\text{gas}} - C_R R \rho_*
\label{eq:lagrangian}
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $Q$: Scalar field coupling to gas with $C_Q = +2.82$
    \item $R$: Scalar field coupling to stars with $C_R = -0.72$
    \item $V(Q,R)$: Potential including $Q$-$R$ interaction terms
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Physical Mechanism}

The U-shape emerges from the competition:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Voids:} Low density, gas-rich $\rightarrow$ $Q$ dominates $\rightarrow$ positive residuals
    \item \textbf{Field:} Intermediate density $\rightarrow$ $Q \approx R$ $\rightarrow$ minimal residuals
    \item \textbf{Clusters:} High density, gas-poor $\rightarrow$ $R$ dominates $\rightarrow$ positive residuals (for gas-rich) or negative (for gas-poor high-mass)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Connection to Fundamental Physics: a line of inquiry that did not hold}

The two-field structure of \QOR{} is superficially reminiscent of string compactifications, in which the dilaton and a K\"ahler modulus provide two scalars with distinct couplings to matter. In the December 2025 version of this work we described that resemblance as suggestive, and a companion document attempted to make it quantitative by deriving the cross-coupling $\lambda_{QR}$ from a Type IIB compactification with KKLT moduli stabilisation. The argument concluded that $\lambda_{QR} \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$, and that this matched the value inferred from the data.

That argument does not survive computation. Evaluating the cross-coupling from the moduli stabilisation potential itself, rather than assuming its magnitude, gives values in the range $10^{-11}$ to $10^{-3}$ across the compactification scenarios examined - between eight and fifteen orders of magnitude below the value the resemblance was taken to explain. The agreement previously reported was not obtained from the compactification; it was presupposed and then found.

We therefore withdraw the claim entirely and do not pursue this line here. What remains is a structural analogy with no quantitative content: two scalars with opposite matter couplings arise naturally in several settings, string compactifications among them, and that observation constrains nothing. The framework as used in this document is phenomenological throughout, and the peer-reviewed article makes no claim of a derivation from fundamental theory. The historical material, including the attempted derivation, is preserved unchanged under \texttt{legacy/} in the repository, so that the reasoning and its failure can be examined.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Astrophysical Alternatives}

Could standard astrophysical processes explain the U-shape?

\textbf{Ram pressure stripping:} Removes gas in clusters, potentially affecting dynamics. However, this cannot explain elevated residuals in voids, and cannot produce the inverted U-shape in gas-poor systems.

\textbf{Tidal effects:} Strong in clusters, negligible in voids. Cannot explain void enhancement.

\textbf{Selection effects:} Different selections in SPARC vs. ALFALFA, yet both show U-shape.

\textbf{Environmental quenching:} Would predict monotonic trends, not U-shape.

\subsection{Why The Discriminating Prediction Matters}

The inverted U-shape in gas-poor, high-mass systems is particularly significant:
\begin{itemize}
    \item It was \textit{predicted} before examining TNG300 data
    \item It requires a sign change that standard physics does not naturally produce
    \item It emerges automatically from the antagonistic $Q$-$R$ structure
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Environmental proxies are imperfect; direct density measurements would strengthen the case
    \item The amplitude difference between SPARC and ALFALFA requires explanation
    \item The theoretical framework is phenomenological; derivation from first principles is needed
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions and Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

This paper documents a conceptual motivation from failed prediction to robust discovery:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Falsification:} The single-field (QO) model fails to describe SPARC data
    \item \textbf{Discovery:} BTFR residuals show a U-shaped environmental dependence ($a = 1.33 \pm 0.25$ for the 175 environmentally classified galaxies, $p < 10^{-6}$)
    \item \textbf{Replication:} The pattern is confirmed on 21,834 ALFALFA galaxies ($p = 0.0065$)
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} A two-field model (\QOR{}) with antagonistic couplings explains the pattern
    \item \textbf{Discriminating Prediction:} Gas-poor, high-mass systems show inverted U-shape, as predicted
    \item \textbf{Constraints:} \QOR{} satisfies solar system tests through environmental screening
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Open Questions}

Several questions motivate further investigation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Is the trend robust to the definition of environment?} This is the most consequential open question. The amplitude and significance reported here are conditional on the catalogue-based structure-membership classification, and weaken under fixed-radius density proxies. A homogeneous re-analysis using nearest-neighbour densities and halo-based classifications is required before the trend can be considered environmentally robust.
    \item \textbf{Can a forward model be derived?} Without an explicit mapping from the parameters of the framework to the fitted curvature, the observational analysis tests the presence of a non-monotonic trend rather than the framework itself. Deriving that mapping is the step that would make the framework quantitatively falsifiable.
    \item \textbf{What is the cosmological evolution of the U-shape?} High-redshift surveys may reveal time dependence.
    \item \textbf{Can resolved observations distinguish \QOR{} from alternatives?} Next-generation IFU surveys will be decisive.
\end{itemize}

Two questions raised in the December 2025 version have since been closed rather than left open. Whether the framework could be derived from string theory was answered in the negative by direct computation (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). Whether the residual-diagnostic method extended to other domains, explored on clinical data in a companion document, was never validated and is not pursued; that material is preserved under \texttt{legacy/}.

\subsection{Concluding Remarks}

The BTFR has long been considered a simple power law with random scatter. Our analysis suggests the scatter contains systematic structure encoding information about environmental physics. Whether this structure reflects new fundamental fields or unrecognized astrophysical processes, it warrants further investigation.

The \QOR{} framework provides a unified description of the observed patterns and makes specific, falsifiable predictions. We present it not as established physics, but as a working hypothesis to guide future observations and theoretical development.
